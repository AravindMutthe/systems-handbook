## Detailed explanation

### Government and public policy

Government and regulators work through policy,
legislation, and enforcement. That work creates the
regulatory and legal environment in which organizations
operate.

### Regulatory and legal environment

The regulatory environment is not a single rule. It
varies by type, by consistency, by speed, by
effectiveness, by enforcement, and by clarity. A
regulation that is consistent and clear does not act
in the same way as one that is inconsistent or unclear,
even if both carry the same title.

In a special report noted on the original page,
government regulation are to the top as the most
influential external factor behind an organization and
workforce ability to thrive. The report itself is not
named on the page, and no further detail about it is
given there.

### Organizations and employers

Organizations and employers act inside that regulatory
environment. Their concerns include hiring, wages, and
safety, and also compliance and investment. Compliance
is the cost and work of meeting regulation. Investment
is the decision to put resources into people, equipment,
and operations under those rules.

### Employment relations and workforce

Employment relations are the relationship between
employer and employee. That relationship runs in both
directions. Employers decide hiring, wages, and safety
conditions. Employees bring rights, skills, and
security, and their motivation and well-being shape
how the relationship works in practice.

Government action could all influence the worker,
employer relationship. That influence does not run
only through a single rule to a single party. It runs
through the regulatory environment to both employer
and employee, and through the relationship between
them.

### Performance, productivity and well-being

Workforce and organizational outcomes are described
as productivity, retention, and well-being. Productivity
is what the workforce produces. Retention is whether
people stay. Well-being is whether people thrive, in
the same word the original page uses for workforce
ability to thrive.

### How the chapter is organized, and how the
### relationships actually work

The chapter is organized as a hierarchy, top to bottom:
Government and public policy, then Regulatory and legal
environment, then Organizations and employers, then
Employment relations and workforce, then Performance,
productivity and well-being.

That hierarchy is for organizing the chapter. The real
relationships are interconnected, and involve feedback
in both directions. Outcomes do not end the story.
Productivity, retention, and well-being feed back into
what employers decide, what employees expect, and what
governments and regulators do next. A hierarchy that
is read as a one-way chain would misread the chapter.


### Standards, coordination, and regulation are distinct

Regulation is not one hierarchy. Regional Internet
registries allocate number resources by region. The
Internet Engineering Task Force develops Internet
technical standards. The Institute of Electrical and
Electronics Engineers develops standards across
electrical, electronics, and computing fields. The
3rd Generation Partnership Project develops mobile
telecommunications specifications. National regulators
such as the Federal Communications Commission and the
Telecom Regulatory Authority of India have national
regulatory roles in their respective jurisdictions.
Each of these does a different job, and none of them
is the same job as a government setting labour policy
or an employer setting hiring policy.

## Interdisciplinary lenses

This chapter is interdisciplinary. Its lenses are:
Business administration, labor economics, HR
management, organizational behavior, and regulatory
policy. Those are disciplines and perspectives on the
same chapter, not separate domains and not separate
cards. The chapter stays in one place, with those
lenses as cross-links, not as duplicate copies.
