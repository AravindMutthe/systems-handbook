Synthesis — A to K

A story in one line, from the cards themselves: A asks why networks exist — business need. B names and addresses them — directory to DNS, with regulation. C supplies the hardware — silicon to vendors to the NIC endpoint. D runs that endpoint — kernel stack and a separate management lane. E builds, operates, and approves the product that uses all of it. F makes that product universal — four parallel flows and a full value chain. G asks who controls and who builds — government layer, global oversight, government networks. H changes the interface era by era, from telegraph keys to real-time, XR, and AI. I asks who leads each era — org structure, tech strategy, leadership mindset, completed after 2000. J reads the same six eras through three lenses — interface, control, and how it is paid for. K schedules it all — tasks and resources, allocated fairly, by whoever owns the allocation.

No new era, body, or claim is introduced here. It is only the eleven cards, in learning order, as one system: ground base, naming, hardware, endpoint, product, universal product, control, interface, leadership, eras, and scheduler. A reader who has read A to K has followed the whole story; a reader who filters by a technical perspective — institutions, hardware, infrastructure, business, software engineering — follows the same story through that lens, without a duplicate card.
