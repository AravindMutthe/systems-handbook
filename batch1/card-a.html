Why networks exist: origins and business need

Ground base: why, before what.

Systems Evolution — a ground-base card: why networks exist, before what they are. It connects business, institutions, and hardware, because a network is never only a device.

Detailed explanation

1. Before networks: signalling, in three steps

  • Networks did not start with computers.
  • Human to human: oral language, signals, written text.
  • Mechanical-signaling systems: fire beacons, drums.
  • Electronic: the telegraph.
  • Each step is the same idea carried further: a message that has to travel farther, faster, or to more people than a voice in one room can reach.
  • This card comes first: it answers why before it answers what.
  • A network exists because signalling had to scale.
  • Humans have always used signal fires, smoke signals, and drums to send messages farther than a voice can be heard.
  • The telegraph carried that same idea over wires.
  • DARPA, the US defence research agency created in 1958, is a later step in that same line — its ARPANET became the basis of the Internet — not the starting point.

2. Business = exchange of information

  • Business = exchange of information.
  • Speed of business = trading.
  • Speed of trading = exchange of information.
  • A deal is only as fast as the information behind it.
  • Faster information → faster trading → faster business.
  • Organisations, rules and coordination appear before any device — they are the conditions that let information move at trading speed.

3. Business organisation, under five rule layers

  • Ownership, board, investors.
  • Executive team, C-suite.
  • Middle management, departments.
  • Under five rule layers: tech standards; governance and coordination; national and international regulation; privacy and security; market rules.
  • The layers are not decoration — they are what the organisation operates under.

4. Regulation, coordination, trust — and government-funded projects

  • Regulation, monopolies.
  • Coordination (IETF).
  • Trust and security.
  • Government-funded physical network and infrastructure projects.
  • A network that only moves information, without coordination, trust and rules, does not stay usable at trading speed.
  • Regulation and coordination sit in this ground-base card — they are part of why networks exist, not an afterthought.

Summary:

  • Networks started with people signalling, then mechanical signals, then the telegraph.
  • Business needed them because a deal is only as fast as the information behind it.
  • That need is why organisations, rules and coordination appear before any device in this sequence.

Key points

  • Before agencies of networks: human to human (oral language, signals, written text), then mechanical-signaling systems (fire beacons, drums), then electronic — telegraph.
  • (1960) DARPA, US Defense Agency network, as written on the page.
  • Business = exchange of information; speed of business = trading; speed of trading = exchange of information.
  • Business organisation: ownership, board, investors, executive team, C-suite, middle management, departments — under five rule layers: tech standards, governance and coordination, national and international regulation, privacy and security, market rules.
  • Regulation, monopolies, coordination (IETF), trust and security, government-funded physical network and infrastructure projects.
